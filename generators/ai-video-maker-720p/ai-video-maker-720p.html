How It Works

This app was designed by @MusicalMuse444 on YouTube and KamTape.

Overview: This tool converts your audio files into dynamic, music-reactive videos using HTML5 Canvas and the Web Audio API. It records the visual output in real-time as the media plays. The browser window must be left open during recording — closing or switching away from the tab will interrupt the process. For videos longer than 10 minutes, full screen mode is recommended to prevent your mobile device screen from timing out and shutting off.

1. Upload Music (Optional): Select one or multiple audio files (MP3, WAV, etc.) to drive the animations. If you select multiple files, they will play sequentially with a customizable silence duration between them. If you don't upload any media, you can specify a duration to create a silent video or slideshow.

2. Background Layering: You have two options for the background, which prioritize in this order:
- Upload Image: If you provide an image (or multiple images), it covers the background. You can now set a specific duration for how long each image is displayed. If the video is longer than the total image cycle, they will loop.
- AI Generation: If no image is uploaded, you can type a prompt (e.g., "Cyberpunk City") to generate an AI art background. You can also select the aspect ratio for this AI image (16:9, Square, or Portrait). You can set a "Display Duration per Image" and the AI will generate enough images to fill your entire video timeline. Regenerate: After generation, you can review all images and regenerate individual ones you don't like using the same prompt.
- Custom Color: If neither of the above is used, the app uses a solid color (default black, customizable via color picker).
Note: The "Default" visualization style adds particle effects on top of whatever background you choose.

3. Text Overlays (The UI):
- Top Left: Song Name.
- Top Right: Credits.
- Bottom Left/Right: Custom text for calls to action (e.g., "Subscribe").
These are static overlays that stay on screen.

4. Centered Text Events:
- Intro Text: Large text in the center at the start. You can enable "Fade out" to make it disappear between 5s and 10s.
- Outro Text: Appears 10 seconds before the video ends, fading in over 5 seconds (e.g., "Thanks for listening"). This only works if the Intro fade-out is enabled.

5. Visualization Styles: Choose how the music looks:
- Default: Center bar spectrum + exploding particles on bass hits.
- Radial: A circular spectrum radiating from the middle.
- Waveform: A landscape-style line graph.
- Orbital: Geometric rings that pulse and rotate with the beat.

5b. Single Visualization Color: By default the visualization cycles through a rainbow of colors. Enable "Use Single Visualization Color" to pick one color for all bars, particles, rings, and glows. Your choice is remembered between visits.

6. Aspect Ratio: Determines the shape of the video file.
- 16:9: Standard for YouTube.
- 9:16: Vertical for TikTok/Reels/Shorts.
- 1:1: Square for Instagram.
- 4:3: Classic TV format.

7. Typography: Select distinct fonts for the "Main Text" (corners) and "Center Text" (intro/outro) to match your vibe.

8. Screen Shake: Adds a dynamic camera shake effect that reacts to the bass. This makes the video feel more energetic and impactful. Text overlays remain steady for readability.

9. Generation Process: Click "Start Visualization & Record". The app plays the media from start to finish (or runs for the specified duration if no media). Do not close the tab or switch windows, as the browser needs to actively render the frames. The recording captures the canvas exactly as you see it.

10. Download: Once the track finishes (or duration elapses), a download button appears. The file is in .webm format, which is high quality and widely supported by browsers and editors (like Premiere/DaVinci).

11. Ready for YouTube: Your videos are ready to upload to YouTube right away as soon as they are created. They render at 720p quality, giving you a crisp, clear result suitable for YouTube uploads. While some social media platforms don't support .webm files, YouTube does — so you can publish directly without any conversion.
